Single-Item Reliability for Intensive Longitudinal Data: A Three-Level Linear Mixed-Effect Autoregressive Modeling Approach

Lin, T.-Y., Tuerlinckx, F., & Vanbelle, S.

Psychometrika

DOI

Show abstract

With the advancement of technology, intensive longitudinal data are increasingly collected to study dynamic psychological and physiological processes. This article introduces a three-level linear mixed-effects model that accounts for moments nested within days within persons and incorporates moment- and day-level autoregressive processes. The framework distinguishes between-person and within-person reliability and assesses measurement agreement across different time scales.

Incorporating the Luce–Krantz threshold model into cultural consensus theory for ordinal categorical data

Lin, T.-Y., & Hsu, Y.-F.

Journal of Mathematical Psychology

DOI

Show abstract

Cultural consensus theory traditionally models dichotomous responses when the culturally correct answers are unknown. This study extends the general Condorcet model with Luce–Krantz threshold theory so that respondents can express varying confidence through ordinal categories. The resulting hierarchical Bayesian model estimates consensus truths, item difficulty, informant competence, guessing bias, and the number of latent cultures.

The impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on vaccine sentiment among Brazilian healthcare workers: A cross-sectional online survey study

Lo, C., Lin, T.-Y., Werneck, G. L., Domingues, C., Saavedra, R. da C., Moore, M., Souza, L. E. P. F., Regazzi, L., & Lomazzi, M.

Journal of Global Health Economics and Policy, 5, e2025034

DOI

Related post

Show abstract

This cross-sectional online survey examined vaccine sentiment among 634 Brazilian healthcare workers. Vaccine hesitancy was uncommon and overall sentiment was positive. Trust in institutional websites was associated with more positive vaccine sentiment, while obligated collective responsibility emerged as a useful extension of the 5C model.

Interaction between coffee consumption and polygenic risk score in relation to diabetes: Insights from The Maastricht Study

Rao, Y., Yu, E. Y.-W., Lin, T.-Y., Chen, Y., Qin, Y., Koster, A., Eussen, S. J. P. M., Bosma, H., Berendschot, T. J. M., van der Kallen, C., van Greevenbroek, M., de Galan, B., Zeegers, M., & Wesselius, A.

European Journal of Nutrition, 64, 263

DOI

Related post

Show abstract

This study investigated the associations of polygenic risk score and habitual coffee consumption with prediabetes and type 2 diabetes among 7,668 participants in the southern Netherlands. No significant interaction between genetic risk and coffee consumption was observed. Coffee consumption showed a potentially U-shaped association with the prevalence of both conditions.

Reliability for multilevel data: A correlation approach

Lin, T.-Y., Tuerlinckx, F., & Vanbelle, S.

Psychological Methods

DOI

Show abstract

This article proposes reliability coefficients for multilevel data based on the expected correlation between repeated measurements. The approach is compared with existing generalizability-theory definitions across common nested designs, clarifying why competing definitions can produce inconsistent cluster-level reliability estimates.

Consistency of perceptual response variability in size estimation and reproduction tasks

Yu, K., Lin, T.-Y., Zaman, J., Tuerlinckx, F., & Vanhasbroeck, N.

Behavior Research Methods, 57(5), 127

DOI

Show abstract

This study compared visual analog scale estimation and reproduction tasks as measures of perceptual uncertainty. Across 180 participants, both tasks produced consistent overall measures, while multilevel variance decomposition and psychophysical modeling revealed systematic task-specific differences in how perceptual variability was expressed.
